## FOREWORD

PCSB PMMS Work Management Execution (PWME) is an integrated maintenance work management framework and process reference designed to provide a structured and systematic approach to managing maintenance activities focusing short-term maintenance planning and execution with linking to long-term maintenance strategies and asset management objectives. The integrated and forward-looking approach promotes process alignment, enhances coordination across functions, and optimises the utilisation of resources, systems, and tools to achieve safe, reliable, and cost-effective operations.

PWME also provides a comprehensive overview of the methods, processes, and requirements underpinning the PWME framework. It establishes the minimum standards necessary to ensure maintenance activities are planned and executed effectively and efficiently. It supports the standardisation of maintenance practices, promotes consistency, and reinforces value-driven decision-making throughout the PWME processes. These principles help ensure that maintenance efforts are aligned with business objectives while maximising value delivery and managing operational risks.

PWME incorporates various work planning practices, among others is the centralised maintenance planning and execution, that promotes cross-functional collaboration, integrated planning, and coordinated scheduling, ensuring effective deployment of maintenance resources while enhancing operational efficiency and consistency across assets. In addition, this manual references other supporting documents relevant for maintenance work management execution and asset integrity, including the Asset Management Procedure and Asset Integrity Findings (AIF), to provide a comprehensive framework and reference on planning, execution, and governance of maintenance activities.

This manual is to be read together with PCSB PMMS Data Minimum Standard Guideline and the PCSB PMMS Manual, the latter serves as the overarching reference document for overall PMMS governance and implementation within PCSB.

## 1.0 INTRODUCTION

### 1.1 Objective

This document is intended to establish a standardised approach for PCSB PMMS Work Management Execution (PWME) to support effective and systematic workflow of maintenance work management practices towards achieving high asset reliability, availability and integrity. It is to be read together with PCSB PETRONAS Maintenance Management System (PMMS) manual that provide general reference for PCSB PMMS particularly on governance and implementation, and PCSB PMMS Data Minimum Standard manual that details out minimum standard and requirement of data setup in PMMS.

### 1.2 Scope

This PWME manual is applicable for PCSB Assets subscribing to standard PMMS – SAP Plant Maintenance (PM) Module. It covers the end-to-end maintenance workflow, covering work identification, work planning, work scheduling, work execution, work close out and analyse & improve.

ii. "Should" indicates a preferred course of action; and

## 4.0 PWME GENERAL OVERVIEW

### 4.1 Overview

PMMS is a software supported system to manage physical assets within EMPHASISE that is based on SAP Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) software, specific for Plant Maintenance Module (also known as I2R – Issue to Resolution). The current SAP ERP Version is S/4HANA, beginning April 2026.

Under the Upstream Operational Excellence Management System (OEMS) for Physical Asset Management (PAM) Value Driver, PMMS tie to two core element i.e Work Planning and Scheduling (WPS) and Computerised Maintenance Management System (CMMS), as depicted in below Figure 4.1. In achieving OEMS objectives on Safe, Reliable and Cost effectiveness, WPS & CMMS emphasise the importance role of implementing PMMS in PAM, via embracing standardized requirement and processes for continuous improvement (refer figure 4.2).

## 4.2 PWME Objective

The objectives of this document are as follows:

i. To support and enhance the maintenance management process through effective Work Planning and Scheduling

ii. To standardise maintenance work process methodology

iii. To define Roles and Responsibilities between maintenance stakeholders

iv. To provide basic information and guide for systematic workflow of maintenance work throughout all key stages

v. To establish communication of work implementation, planning strategy and information sharing between Maintenance, Operation, and other disciplines

vi. To enable coordinated and collaborative work planning with seamless integration and less overlaps between major planning functions

## 4.3 PWME Implementation

PWME implementation involves all stages of maintenance activity; it builds and consolidates existing processes into one standard and shall be used as a standard practice for all PCSB facilities. The basis of PWME implementation strategies are:

i. **The Process: Lean & Mean** – To enable "Get - It- Right" upfront by performing the right work, at the right time and with the right resources

ii. **The Implementation: Comprehensive Groundwork** – To facilitate thorough preparation which ensures efficient execution and no wastage of resources

iii. **The Transaction: Excellent Utilisation of PMMS** – To ensure accuracy and standardisation of maintenance planning and execution data

i. Accurate work scope and priority

## 5.0 ROLES & RESPONSIBILITIES

The two core roles and responsibilities, and competency requirements under PWME are:

i. Planning Team
ii. Execution Team

### 5.1 Planning Team

Responsible for the planning & preparation stage. The planning team will plan & schedule accordingly based on the received work scope.

#### 5.1.1. Operation & Maintenance Manager

Key responsibilities:

i. Accountable for good work planning & preparation
ii. Support the region and area operation supervisor and engineer
iii. Monitor trends as well as review and discuss improvement strategies
iv. Ensure adequacy of budget, contracts and resources
v. Interface with stakeholders such as partners and supporting department

Competency Requirement (specific to PWME):

i. Awareness of PCSB PMMS Work Management Execution
ii. Ability to manage risk and issue escalation related to PWME processes

#### 5.1.2. Field Engineer

Key responsibilities:

i. Develop and cascade high level planning (from ARP/MRP)
ii. Continuous updating & realignment of the long-term schedule
iii. Realignment of activities inside IAP

Competency Requirement:

i. Awareness of PCSB PMMS Work Management Execution
ii. Ability to manage risk and issue escalation related to PWME processes

#### 5.1.3. Planner

Key responsibilities:

i. Ensure effective planning and preparation of work
ii. Diagnose and troubleshoot cause of site issues
iii. Identify scope of work and develop work pack
iv. Consolidate feedback and lessons learnt from completed activity for continuous improvement of work pack development

Commonly maintenance works are categorized under two major types:

i. **Preventive Maintenance (PM)**: is a proactive approach designed to prevent equipment failure before it occurs. It is a maintenance strategy regardless commonly with predetermined intervals.
ii. **Corrective Maintenance (CM)**: refers to any maintenance activity required to correct a failure that has occurred or is in the process of occurring. This activity may consist of repair, restoration, or replacement of components. It is a reactive approach, addressing issues after they have been detected.

In PMMS application, maintenance works approaches are mainly on below:

i. **Proactive maintenance** is a maintenance strategy that focuses on anticipating, preventing, and mitigating equipment failures through planned inspection, monitoring, and maintenance activities before failures occur. The objective is to improve asset reliability, optimize lifecycle costs, and maximize operational availability.
ii. **Reactive maintenance** is a maintenance strategy in which maintenance activities are initiated only after equipment has failed or its performance has deteriorated to an unacceptable level. The primary objective is to restore the equipment to its required operating condition as quickly as possible.

## 6.2.5. Risk Assessment and Work Prioritisation

Notification and Work Order Priority shall use the HSE Risk Matrix for Work Prioritisation (RMP). This provides a common and consistent basis for all requests. The primary consideration is business risk to People, Asset, Environment and Reputation (PEAR), which should be kept at as low as reasonably practicable (ALARP). Subsequent consideration is on 'Consequence' on PEAR and 'Likelihood' of effect escalating without intervention. The overall Risk, determined by Impact × Likelihood, determines the work priority. This is further illustrated in Figure 6.4.

The overall priority in PMMS is auto-calculated based on Consequence Category (PEAR), Consequence Class & Probability Class (Likelihood) determined by the requestor.

Work prioritisation aims to:

i. Establish a common understanding of business risk if repair work is not executed

ii. Rank the work to ensure the highest priority work is performed first

iii. Allow consistency of quantifying the priority of work taking into consideration Production and Maintenance risk factors

Through the prioritisation process, it is possible to identify safety or production-related emergency work that is high priority. This will allow non-emergency work to be considered as lower priority and hence, executed at a later time. During Risk Assessment of CM requests, Operational state of equipment, including any related defects awaiting repair or operational constraints, and mitigation actions carried out or to be taken, should be considered.

It is essential that the final prioritisation is the result of a team discussion that has considered all possible factors. Allocating a low priority does not mean that the work will not be planned. It merely defines its relative importance and determines its work completion date. These jobs may be executed earlier depending on opportunity.

Risk prioritisation will determine Latest Allowable Finish Date (LAFD) as per Figure 6.4. However, this is more to assisting on the planning for work completion date. The work prioritisation still shall be subject to above Consequences and Likelihood.

Over time, the Work Pack should form a Job Library, where it can be re-used across Assets, with minor updates to suit the relevant activity. A work pack is useful for the following reasons:

i. Enables accurate and efficient scheduling/assignment of the work

ii. For operators to know how long an equipment will remain unavailable

iii. For determining what work could be carried out in a short outage situation

An assessment of the risks and complexity of work should be made. For each safety concern, there should be:

i. A Job Hazard Analysis (JHA)

ii. An evaluation of cumulative risks from nearby activities such as hot work and working from height

iii. Specific data sheets or check lists for use by the executor, e.g. chemical data sheets or instrument check lists

A Work Pack shall be as follows:

i. Develop detailed operation tasks and manhours required (refer to task list for recurring job)

ii. Prepare applicable work pack, repair procedures and associated checklists

iii. Prepare Permit to Work (PTW)

iv. Identify special tools, if required

v. Identify relevant drawings, if required

vi. Prepare Job Safety Analysis (JSA), if required

Work hours and duration would initially be made based on experience, but over time reference should be made from similar works in the Job Library. The man-hours planned should include approximated hours required by skilled executors without non-productive time added. This means only wrench time should be planned.

The Permit to Work (PTW) standard should be used as guidance on the requirement of planning, procedures and approvals needed to execute the work.

## 6.3.9. Material

Maintenance work may involve consumption of materials. This shall be identified in the Work Pack for each task. Material can be selected from:

i. **Bills of Materials (BOMs)** – a list of spare parts associated with an equipment type or tag number

ii. **Material catalogues** – a list of all stocked and non-stocked materials

Priority of material searching should start with catalogued materials such as coded and inventoried materials. Materials that are not catalogued (e.g. generic materials) should be the later option as this requires supplier selection and negotiation before they can be ordered.

Materials that are not currently catalogued, should be initiated and evaluated for cataloguing requirements, particularly for materials intended for recurring use, inventory management, or strategic spare holding. ## 6.3.14. Work Breakdown Structure (WBS) Element

Work Order cost (e.g. costs for material, personnel, external services) are initially collected at the work order and transferred to the settlement receiver (e.g. Cost Center, WBS, Asset, etc.) specified in the settlement rule.

The registered WBS will be automatically defaulted to Work Order cost settlement according to the selected Functional Location; thus facilitating work planning process. ## 6.4.6. Backlog Management

Backlog is the list of maintenance work that remains outstanding in PMMS and must be managed according to its priority, readiness, constraints, and risk. There are two categories of backlog, Active and Inactive backlog.

i. **Active Backlog**

a. Work that is ready to be scheduled or executed

b. Normally includes work orders that are already prepared and can move into the schedule

c. This is the main focus of backlog management

ii. **Inactive Backlog**

a. Work that unable to proceed because of constraints

b. Example constraints as per Table 6.5:

| SAP User Status | User Status Description | When to Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AWSD | Awaiting Shutdown | Work needs to be postponed until next shutdown |
| AWTA | Awaiting Turnaround | Work to be performed during planned turnaround |
| AWMT | Awaiting Material | Work delayed due to non-availability of material or waiting for supply from vendor |
| AWMP | Awaiting Manpower | Work delayed due to non-availability of internal and/or external manpower |
| SUSP | Work Suspended | Work suspended due to manpower reallocated for other urgent work which requires full attention from maintenance. |

c. Once the constraint is removed, the work order becomes active again

## 6.5.7. Record Result for SCE Maintenance Work Orders

For completed SCE maintenance work, assurance tasks shall be performed to verify compliance with Performance Standards to preserve equipment integrity and reliability.

Inspection Lots are automatically generated upon work order release, and all test results must be accurately recorded and reviewed prior to entry into PMMS. All results shall be entered based on its quantitative or qualitative criteria. For qualitative criteria, result must be set to 'Y' for accepted (allowable), or 'N' for rejected (not allowable). For quantitative criteria, the entered value shall be matched against Specification retrieved from the Performance Standard Classification of the SCE Functional Location, that is 'N" for rejected (value is beyond specification) or 'Y' for accepted (value is within the specification).

Upon completion of results and defect recording (if any) in PMMS, the system will prompt for Usage Decision for the Inspection Point (combination of Operation and Functional Location). If all results are accepted, "Passed" will be the default system proposed. If there are one or more rejected results, "Failed" would be the default system proposed.

"Failed" work shall need to be acted upon by the raising of a corrective work notification. Changing of the default system proposed is allowed, for example, from "Failed" to "Failed and Fixed". The signed-off Performance Standards must be attached in the work order for reference and record purposes. Otherwise, it shall be kept in common filing or folder for future reference.

For work performed by Contractors, the Discipline Engineer or Person in Charge (PIC) shall review the work done. After review, it shall be approved by relevant Managers and recorded in system by PIC.

## 6.6.9. KP26 Labour Rate Maintenance

PMMS Focal Person shall maintain the labour rate (T-code KP26) with reference to activity types for cost center used in work center at every beginning of the new year. Cost center and work center association allows the costs of maintenance activities performed at the work center to be accurately captured and allocated to the appropriate cost center.

Activity types categorize the activities performed by cost centers (e.g., labor hours). Each activity type can have its own rate which helps in calculating the cost of the activity. Currently in PETRONAS, below activity type value maintained in work center:

i. S01 – Internal labour
ii. C01 – External labour

Maintaining accurate labor rates in SAP enables:

i. Accurately calculate maintenance costs.
ii. Allocate labor expenses to the correct maintenance orders.
iii. Support budgeting and cost planning.
iv. Measure maintenance cost performance.

**Prerequisites:**

i. Work centers and cost centers have been created.
ii. Internal labour rate is calculated using following formula:

3 series costs for maintenance department

(((52 weeks * 39 hours) + 15% planned overtime hours) – ((20 days leave + 12 training days + 20 holidays)* 8 hours)) * Number of maintenance technicians

> **Note:** The denominator in the formula above can be substituted with Soloman manhours if available. The numerator 3 series cost elements are all the cost elements related to labour/OPU manpower costs.

Following errors are expected if this is not maintained:

i. Error for settlement rule during work order processing
ii. Timesheet entry error
