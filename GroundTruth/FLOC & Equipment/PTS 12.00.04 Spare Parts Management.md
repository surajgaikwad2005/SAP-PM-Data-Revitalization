## 1.0 INTRODUCTION

This PTS provides the minimum technical requirements and recommendations for the identification, selection, purchase and setting up spare parts quantity/stock level for new projects, including modification/revamp project for smooth operation and maintenance. This PTS incorporates PETRONAS lesson learnt and best practices.

## 1.2 SCOPE

This PTS covers requirement of insurance spares, commissioning spares, initial spares, and operating spares to finalise the quantity/stock level by Owner and Contractor during relevant engineering stage of project. This PTS also defines the responsibilities of the Supplier, Contractor, and the Owner.

The requirements in this PTS is not applicable for spare parts that are managed by vendors or contractors as part of accepted service or support requirements, and spare parts shall not be kept and maintained within the facilities.

**2.0 SPARE PARTS REQUIREMENTS**

The Contractor shall be responsible to comply with this PTS for selection, procurement and supply, documentation, packing, preservation and handing over of the required spare parts to the Owner. i. Insurance Spares
ii. Commissioning Spares & Consumables
iii. Initial Operating Spares

Subsequent sections of this PTS define these categories and the different processes expected to be followed to identify, select, and procure them.

## 2.2 SPARE PARTS PRESERVATION

**2.2.1** Contractor shall communicate with the equipment Manufacturers/OEM/Suppliers to ensure a robust preservation plan are in place i.e. required storage climate (indoor, outdoor, including humidity control/heating element, nitrogen purged arrangements etc..), packaging, and routine verification.

**2.2.2** Contractor shall ensure that each part delivered is well preserved, packed/boxed and Identified with a robust, weatherproof label, showing the:

i. Owner's coding reference as specified in the Purchase Order, Manufacturer/OEM/Supplier's name
ii. Equipment Manufacturer's unique part number: i.e. description of the part, expiry dates for parts having a limited shelf life, or if required for stock maintenance.

**2.2.3** All spare parts shall be preserved as per Manufacturer/OEM/Supplier's instruction or/and in accordance with PTS 15.20.16 as agreed by Owner.

## 2.3 SPARE PARTS QUANTITY SELECTION

Stock Review Committee (SRC) is a platform where stocking proposals (including the quantities) get deliberated. The quorum for SRC comprises of the one (1) chairman, one (1) technical member, one (1) secretary and requestor. This platform deliberates the proposal from finance and operation perspective. SRC serves as a gatekeeper for new proposal of stocks into PETRONAS Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) system.

All proposal to SRC shall be performed after the requestor performs Initial Spare Analytics (iSPARE) or Equipment Reliability Based Stocking (ERBS) analysis with the help of Group Procurement.

### 2.3.1 Initial Spares Analytics (iSPARE)

This PETRONAS-developed tool is an analytical-based algorithm aimed to optimise initial inventory minimum and maximum levels based on the following:

i. Failure rates
ii. Planned consumption rates
iii. Comparison with OEM recommendation

iSPARE is a combination of algorithms that process SAP spare and maintenance historical data from PETRONAS downstream operating units, index each spare, performs cluster of spare and equipment, simulate and prescribe forecast failure probability within specified timeframe (e.g. 2 years, 4 years).

Engineering, Procurement, Construction and Commissioning (EPCC) contractors shall produce PETRONAS Spare Part Interchangeability Record (P-SPIR) documents with the intent to support client's inventory needs. The P-SPIR data shall contain manufacturer's data sheets relevant to equipment listed and complete spare breakdown for each tag number listed with recommended spare quantity to keep.

PETRONAS as client shall review the proposal resulted from P-SPIR document to avoid potential surplus as the plant continues in operation.

Snapshot of iSPARE is provided in Appendix 2.

## 2.3.2 Equipment Reliability Based Stocking (ERBS)

This PETRONAS-developed tool is a simulation-based algorithm aimed to optimise inventory minimum and maximum levels (by targeting to achieve lowest total ownership cost) based on the following:

i. Failure rates
ii. Planned consumption rates
iii. Consequence of spare parts unavailability to operating plant
iv. Lead times
iv. Spare part costs
v. Spare part holding cost

Periodic review of inventory is required during operating phase of the facilities. Some inventories will either eventually become sub-optimum and surplus. ERBS shall be used to provide insight from both finance and operation sense, to propose the needs to decrease or increase the current inventory levels.

ERBS has been filed for patent protection with application no of PI2020000349.

Snapshot of ERBS is provided in Appendix 3 and user guide (with snapshot of template) in Appendix 4.

## 3.0 INSURANCE SPARES

### 3.1 GENERAL

For specific definition for insurance spare, refer to Clause 1.2.2. Insurance spares are generally characterised by having:

i. High purchase cost
ii. Low likelihood of usage
iii. Unpredictable demand
iv. Long lead item or long delivery item (> 6-month delivery time)
v. High operational impact if not available

Insurance Spares may be components or complete units, such as complete pumps, large valves, motors, transformers, turbo-machinery cartridges, rotors, and heat exchanger tube bundles.

Insurance Spares, if required for use under certain circumstances during commissioning, it shall be replaced by Contractor before contract closure as per agreement of Owner.

Any Insurance spares if used during normal operation/maintenance, Owner shall take necessary steps to refurbish the damaged parts/procure such spare, as the case may be, at the earliest so that its unavailability shall not affect the plant operation.

### 3.2 ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITY

3.2.1 Refer to Para 7.0, Table 7.1 for the roles and responsibilities matrix for each category of spare parts.

### 3.3 SELECTION AND QUANTITY

3.3.1 Insurance Spares are typically derived from the Reliability, Availability and Maintainability (RAM) study and Equipment Criticality Assessment (ECA).

3.3.2 Insurance Spares selection and quantity shall be derived using ERBS.

## 3.4 PROCUREMENT

3.4.1 Authorising any orders for Insurance Spares shall require agreement of the Owner. The Insurance Spares should be procured simultaneously with the parent equipment, since this shall be more economical and enable complete Factory Acceptance Tests. Hence, their requirement should be identified before long lead item procurement starts.

## 3.5 COLLECTION

3.5.1 All Insurance Spares in P-SPIR under Commissioning Spares shall be listed by the Manufacturer/Supplier. This shall facilitate monitoring and cataloguing and prevent duplicate purchasing of these expensive spares.

3.5.2 The Contractor shall confirm the inclusion of all Insurance Spares in P-SPIR prior to forwarding to the Owner.

## 3.6 STORAGE AND HANDOVER

3.6.1 The Contractor shall be responsible to receive the Insurance spare and proper storage and preservation at site until handing over to Owner.

3.6.2 The Contractor shall maintain the Insurance Spares as per preservation instructions and ensure that all preservation documentation is handed over to the Owner. The Contractor shall be accountable for losses/damage to the Insurance Spares up to the point of handover to the Owner.

3.6.3 The Owner shall define the handover process for Insurance Spare Parts.

## 3.7 DELIVERABLES

The Contractor shall deliver:

i. Insurance Spares Recommendation to Owner
ii. Completed P-SPIR for insurance spare parts
iii. Supporting Documentation
iv. Insurance Spare Parts, labelled according to the Owner's coding requirements

## 4.0 COMMISSIONING SPARES & CONSUMABLES

### 4.1 GENERAL

For specific definition for Commissioning Spares & consumable, refer to Clause 1.2.2.
Requirement of Commissioning Spares are driven by following factors:

i. During transportation
   a. Damage may occur during transport to site

ii. Weather and environmental
   a. Exposure to weather and dirty environment

iii. During testing and initial start-up
   a. Abnormal operating conditions, which increased use and premature wear/failure

iv. Equipment
   a. Standing idle for long periods may be subject to deterioration
   b. Testing and functional checking, with frequent opening for inspection
   c. Damage cause by cleaning and de-preservation activities
   d. Little or no operating experience

**4.1.1** Commissioning Spares typically consist of start-up elements, filter elements, seals, joints, electrical and instrumentation components, calibration equipment, special tools, pipe, fittings, bolts, gaskets, electrical fuses, bulbs, cable, instrument tubing/fittings, lubricants, oils, chemicals, preservation fluids, recording paper, chemical drying agents, etc.

**4.1.2** Consumables such as oils, greases, chemicals, gases, and paints, etc. shall be procured and stocked for a suitable duration as agreed by Owner, for commissioning stage. These materials require special and safe storage in accordance with the applicable HSE requirements. The Contractor shall identify materials requiring additional storage and segregation and ensure that the storage facilities and controls are appropriate.

**4.1.3** The usage of commissioning spares and consumables shall be recorded by Contractor and verified by Owner.

**4.1.4** Leftover commissioning spares shall be recorded and verified by Owner for use as initial spares. Owner has the right to accept or reject leftover spares as initial spares. Owner shall adjust the numbers/quantity of initial spares accordingly. The accepted spare parts "as initial Spares" shall be registered as per PETRONAS inventory management process.

## 5.0 INITIAL OPERATING SPARES

### 5.1 GENERAL

For specific definition for Initial Operating Spares refer to Clause 1.2.2. Initial Operating Spares requirements are driven by specific factors which are particular to initial operations. For instance:

i. Unknown/untested equipment
ii. Increased load on equipment (e.g., high throughput)
iii. Increased potential for trips and subsequent starts may lead to wear and tear
iv. Unexpected process conditions (e.g., sand production)

5.1.1 Initial Operating Spares overlap in some areas with Commissioning Spares and Operating Spares. They consist typically of components such as bearings, mechanical seals, valves, instrument gauges, timers, filter elements, seal repair kits, gaskets, belts, electrical relays, electronic cards, fuel nozzles, etc.

### 5.2 ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES

5.2.1 Refer to Table 7.1 for the roles and responsibilities matrix for each category of spare parts.

### 5.3 SELECTION AND QUANTITY

## 6.0 OPERATING SPARES

## 6.1 GENERAL

6.1.1 For specific definition for Operating Spares refer to Clause 1.2.2. Operating Spares can be categorised as following:

i. Operating spares for corrective or condition-based maintenance
   a. Spares that may fail in use and whose replacement is part of a corrective maintenance action or of an overhaul scheduled as a result of monitoring

ii. Operating spares for preventative maintenance
   a. Spares that deteriorate gradually and are required for scheduled replacement

iii. Kits
   a. For practical reasons, it may be effective to stock complete repair kits. Commercially they are only justifiable if it is expected that the majority of the parts contained shall be used in one overhaul. The purpose of the kit should be well defined and known to the Manufacturer/Supplier (e.g., Turbine Service or Valve Overhaul Kits)
